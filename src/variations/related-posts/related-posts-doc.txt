Related Posts Variation
=======================

What this variation does
------------------------
The **Related Posts** variation quickly creates a section that shows posts related to the one a visitor is currently reading.

It uses WordPress taxonomies (for example, **Categories** and **Tags**) to find posts that are similar.

Typical use:

- At the bottom of a single blog post page.
- On a single post template in a block theme.

Where to find it
----------------
1. Edit a **single post template** (in a block theme) or a post layout where you want to show related posts.
2. Open the block inserter.
3. Search for **“Related Posts”** under Query or Query Loop variations.
4. Insert the **Related Posts** variation.

What you get when you insert it
-------------------------------
When you insert the Related Posts variation, it:

- Adds a **Query** block already configured to show related posts.
- Includes a **Post Template** displaying:
  - Featured image
  - Post title
- Uses a grid layout (for example, 3 columns) so related posts appear in a neat row or grid.
- Includes a “No results” block if no related posts are found.

You can freely customize this layout once it’s inserted.

Choosing which taxonomies to use
--------------------------------
Related posts are determined by matching taxonomies. For example:

- Posts that share the same **Category**.
- Posts that share the same **Tag**.
- Or other custom taxonomies (if your site uses them).

To choose which taxonomies matter:

1. Click the **Related Posts Query** block (the outer block that wraps the grid).
2. Open the block settings in the right sidebar.
3. Look for a **Related Posts** or **Filter by Taxonomies** panel.
4. Use the input (often a token field) to add the taxonomies you care about:
   - Start typing “Categories”, “Tags”, or another taxonomy name.
   - Select the ones you want.
5. Save your changes.

When a visitor views a post, the Related Posts section will try to find other posts that share those taxonomies.

Customizing the layout
----------------------
You can customize the inner layout just like any other Query Loop:

1. Click inside the Related Posts section and select the **Post Template** block.
2. Add or remove blocks inside the template:
   - Add **Post Excerpt** for a short summary.
   - Add **Post Date** or **Post Author**.
   - Wrap items in a Group to add background colors or padding.
3. Adjust the grid layout:
   - Change the number of columns (for example, from 3 to 2 if you want bigger cards).
   - Adjust spacing and alignment in the layout settings.

Ideas for using Related Posts
-----------------------------
- **Classic “You may also like” section**
  - Place Related Posts directly under the main content of each blog post.
  - Show 3 related posts with image and title.

- **Category‑focused suggestions**
  - Configure the variation to use **Categories** only.
  - Visitors reading a post in a category will see other posts from that same category.

- **Tag‑based discovery**
  - For sites using many tags, use **Tags** as the main taxonomy.
  - This can surface posts that are connected by topic even if they are in different categories.

Best practices
--------------
- Ensure posts are well‑categorized and tagged:
  - The better your taxonomy setup, the more relevant your related posts will be.

- Keep the layout simple:
  - Use a clear image, title, and maybe a short excerpt.
  - Avoid overloading the section with too much text.

- Match your theme:
  - Style the Related Posts grid (colors, typography, spacing) so it feels like a natural part of your theme.

Troubleshooting
---------------
- **No related posts appear**
  - Check that the current post actually shares categories/tags with other posts.
  - Try broadening your taxonomies (for example, use Categories instead of a very specific custom taxonomy).

- **The section shows posts that don’t feel related**
  - Review your site’s categories and tags for consistency.
  - Limit the Related Posts taxonomy selection to the ones that make the most sense (for example, only Categories).
