The Table of Contents block automatically generates a list of headings from your page or post content. It scans your content for headings (H1, H2, H3, etc.) and displays them in a structured, clickable list that helps visitors navigate long articles quickly. When visitors click on a heading in the table of contents, the page smoothly scrolls to that section. This is especially useful for long-form content, tutorials, documentation, and articles with multiple sections.

How to add this block

1. Open the editor and navigate to the page or post where you want to add a table of contents. This is typically a long article or page with multiple sections.

2. Click the + button (Add block) in the editor toolbar or within an existing block.

3. Search for "Table of Contents" in the block inserter and click to insert it.

4. Once inserted, the block will automatically scan your page for headings and display them in a list. The block works by detecting heading blocks (Heading, H1, H2, H3, etc.) that appear after it in the content.

5. Position the block where you want it to appear. Most commonly, this is near the top of your content, right after the introduction or opening paragraph, so visitors can see the table of contents before they start reading.

6. The block will show a preview of the headings it has found. You can see how many headings it detected and get a sense of the structure.

7. If you don't see any headings listed, make sure you have Heading blocks in your content that appear after the Table of Contents block. The block only detects headings that come after it in the content flow.

How to style it

Use the style panels in the sidebar to customize the appearance:

- Color: Set background and text colors to match your design. A light background color can make the table of contents stand out as a distinct section. Adjust text colors for good contrast and readability. You can also set link colors for the heading links—typically a brand color or a color that indicates interactivity. Consider using a slightly different color for visited links.

- Typography: Change font size, font family, and font weight to match your content style. Smaller font sizes work well for compact tables of contents, while larger sizes are better if you want it to be more prominent. You can make the title larger and the list items smaller for hierarchy. Adjust line height for better readability, especially if you have many items.

- Border: Add borders and rounded corners to create a card-like appearance. A subtle border can help define the table of contents area. Rounded corners give it a modern, soft look. You can create a boxed appearance with padding and borders, or keep it minimal with just a background color.

- Spacing: Add padding inside the block so the list doesn't touch the edges. This creates breathing room and makes the content easier to scan. Adjust margins above and below to separate it from other content. More margin creates more visual separation from the main content.

- List styling: The block supports different list styles. You can choose between numbered (ordered) and bulleted (unordered) lists. The "Plain Links" style variant provides a simpler, less prominent appearance without traditional list markers.

Settings and options

- Title: Edit the "Table of Contents" title text to whatever you prefer, such as "Contents", "On This Page", "Quick Navigation", or remove it entirely. You can make it match your site's tone—formal, casual, or technical.

- Show Title: Toggle to show or hide the title completely. Hiding the title gives you a cleaner look if the context makes it obvious what the list is for.

- Ordered list: Toggle between numbered (ordered) and bulleted (unordered) list styles. Numbered lists are useful when the order matters or when you want to reference sections by number. Bulleted lists are more casual and work well for general navigation.

- Allowed headings: Choose which heading levels to include in the table of contents. You can select H1, H2, H3, H4, H5, and H6 individually. For example, you might only want H2 and H3 headings to keep the list focused and not too long. This is especially useful for very long articles where including all heading levels would create an overwhelming list. Typically, H2 and H3 are the most useful levels to include.

- Smooth scroll: Enable smooth scrolling animation when visitors click on a heading link. Instead of jumping instantly to the section, the page smoothly scrolls to it, creating a more polished user experience. This is recommended for better UX, though some users with motion sensitivity may prefer instant jumps.

Tips

- Place the Table of Contents near the top of long articles to help visitors quickly find what they're looking for. This is especially important for content over 1000 words or content with many sections.

- Use the "Plain Links" style variant for a simpler, less prominent appearance that doesn't compete with your main content.

- Adjust which heading levels are included to keep the list focused and not too long. Including only H2 and H3 is often sufficient and prevents the list from becoming overwhelming.

- For very long articles, consider using only H2 headings in the table of contents to create a high-level overview. Visitors can then scroll to the section they want and see the H3 subheadings within that section.

- Make sure your headings are descriptive and clear, as they'll appear exactly as written in the table of contents. Good headings help users understand what each section covers.

- Test the smooth scroll feature to ensure it works well with your theme and doesn't conflict with other scroll behaviors.

- Consider the visual hierarchy: Make the title stand out, and ensure the list items are easy to scan. Proper spacing and typography choices make a big difference in usability.

- For accessibility, ensure there's good contrast between the text and background colors, and that the links are clearly identifiable as clickable elements.
